# G-Spot

**Type:** Anatomical Site Register  
**Primary Embodiment:** Female

## Purpose

The G-spot (Gräfenberg spot) refers to an area of heightened sensitivity on the anterior (front) vaginal wall, roughly 5–8 cm inside. It is closely associated with the urethral sponge and Skene’s glands and is often linked to deeper, more internal sensations and specific types of orgasm.

## Allowed Terms

Preferred / precise terms:
- G-spot
- Anterior vaginal wall (more anatomical)
- Urethral sponge area

## Banned Terms

Terms to avoid:
- Overly simplistic or mythologizing language
- Reducing it solely to a “button”

## Somatic Descriptions

How this site typically feels and responds:
- Can produce deep, internal, pressure-based pleasure
- Often described as needing firm, sustained pressure rather than light touch
- May create a sensation of needing to urinate when first stimulated
- Strong connection to full-body and deep pelvic sensation

## Mirror Potential

Sites this area commonly mirrors to:
- Clit (very strong)
- Urethra and urethral sponge
- Pelvic floor
- Deep internal sensation and emotional states

## Propagation Patterns

How sensation or activation from this site tends to move:
- Firm pressure → deep internal sensation → pelvic floor and breath response
- Often creates slower-building, deeper waves compared to clitoral stimulation
- Strong propagation into emotional and full-body states

## Associated Structures

Related or functionally linked anatomical elements:
- Skene’s glands
- Urethral sponge
- Anterior vaginal wall
- Clit (via internal structure)

## Emotional / Symbolic Associations

Common emotional or symbolic meanings:
- Deep surrender and receptivity
- Intensity and overwhelm
- Sometimes linked to emotional release or “female ejaculation” themes

## Notes / Constraints

- One of the more debated but neurologically significant areas
- Requires clear communication and patience in exploration
- Strong mirror connections to clitoris and urethra
- Best understood as part of a broader internal pleasure system rather than an isolated spot

## Related Registers

- Somatic: Deep internal responsiveness

- Mirrors: Strong clitoral and urethral mirrors
- Propagation: Slower, deeper internal propagation pathways
