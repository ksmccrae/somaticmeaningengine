
**Type:** Master Embodiment Layer (Level 0)  
**Status:** Active
## Purpose

The Shared layer represents experiences, sensations, and states that exist across **all** embodiment layers. These are the universal aspects of being human that are not tied to a specific sexed or gendered body, but are still deeply somatic and meaningful.

This layer acts as a foundation that all other embodiment layers can draw from and return to.

### Anatomy & Physiology
- Skin

- Temperature regulation
- Pain perception
- Touch
- Fatigue
### Hormonal & Reproductive States
- General hormonal fluctuations (not sex-specific)
- Stress response systems
- Basic reproductive awareness (as a shared human experience)
### Embodied Experience
- Hunger
- Ageing
- Mortality awareness
- Grief
- Fear
- General physical vulnerability
## Shared Elements

Elements that overlap with other embodiment layers:

- Skin as primary interface
- Breath as constant somatic anchor
- Temperature as felt experience

- Touch and tactile sensation

- Hunger and basic bodily needs
- Ageing processes
- Awareness of mortality
- Emotional residues such as grief and fear
- General physical vulnerability
## Specific Considerations

Unique aspects of this embodiment layer:

- Acts as a common baseline that all other embodiment layers can reference
- Prevents the ontology from becoming overly specialized or fragmented
- Holds experiences that are fundamentally human rather than sexed or gendered
- Serves as a return point when working across multiple embodiment layers
## Relationship to Tropes

How this embodiment layer typically interacts with or selects tropes:

- Tropes often activate or intensify shared somatic experiences (e.g. breath, touch, fatigue, temperature)
- Many tropes work by heightening or ritualizing universal human states rather than sex-specific ones
- Shared elements frequently become the ground on which trope-specific expressions are built
- Tropes can create different *meanings* out of the same shared somatic experience depending on context and embodiment
## Mirror Dynamics

How this layer generates or receives mirror responses:

- Shared somatic states (breath, temperature, fatigue, pain) readily produce cross-embodiment mirror effects
- When one person experiences a universal state (e.g. exhaustion, cold, grief), it can trigger sympathetic resonance in others regardless of their embodiment layer
- The Shared layer often acts as the “common channel” through which mirror responses travel between different embodiment expressions
## Notes

- The Shared layer functions as connective tissue across the entire ontology
- Many mirror and propagation effects originate in or pass through shared somatic states
- This layer should be referenced whenever working across multiple embodiment expressions
- Future expansions may include more detailed mappings of how specific shared states interact with different tropes and registers
