# Male

**Type:** Master Embodiment Layer (Level 0)  
**Status:** Active

## Purpose

The Male layer represents the specific anatomical, physiological, hormonal, and experiential foundations associated with male embodiment. It includes the full spectrum of male bodily experience, from reproductive anatomy to hormonal patterns and their somatic expressions.

## Core Dimensions

### Anatomy & Physiology
- Reproductive anatomy (penis, testicles, prostate)
- Semen production and ejaculation
- Facial and body hair patterns
- Muscle mass and strength distribution
- Prostate and pelvic floor

### Hormonal & Reproductive States
- Testosterone-dominant hormonal patterns
- Daily and age-related hormonal shifts
- Erectile and ejaculatory function
- Prostate health and sensation

### Embodied Experience
- Genital and pelvic sensation
- Ejaculatory experience
- Facial hair growth and maintenance
- Strength, power, and physical capability sensations
- Prostate and perineal awareness

## Shared Elements

Elements that overlap with other embodiment layers:

- Skin, breath, temperature, touch, fatigue, pain, hunger, ageing, mortality
- General emotional and nervous system responses

## Specific Considerations

Unique aspects of this embodiment layer:

- Direct relationship with semen and ejaculatory processes
- Testosterone-influenced somatic and energetic patterns
- Specific genital and prostate mappings
- Often carries cultural meanings around strength, penetration, and release

## Relationship to Tropes

How this embodiment layer typically interacts with or selects tropes:

- Tropes involving semen, ejaculation, penetration, or dominance often route strongly through this layer
- Medical and clinical tropes frequently engage male reproductive anatomy
- Some tropes take on different charge when expressed through testosterone-dominant bodies

## Mirror Dynamics

How this layer generates or receives mirror responses:

- Ejaculatory and prostatic states can produce strong mirror effects
- Genital and pelvic sensations often mirror across embodiments
- Strength, power, and release sensations can create resonant responses in others

## Notes

- The Male layer should be treated with the same precision and respect as other embodiment layers
- Avoid reducing this layer to stereotypes of strength or dominance
- Many shared states are modulated differently through testosterone-dominant physiology
