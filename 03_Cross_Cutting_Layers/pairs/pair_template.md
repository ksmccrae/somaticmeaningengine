# {{Pair Name}}

**Type:** Pair (Cross-Cutting)  
**Function:** Relationship Architecture

## Purpose

Pairs define **who is connected**. They establish the relationship structure between participants, entities, roles, or systems. They describe the *existence* of a relationship, not what happens within it.

## Key Question

Pairs answer:
> "Who exists in relation to whom?"

## Ontological Function

Pairs establish:
- Relationship Context
- Role Structure
- Social Position
- Power Potential
- Witness Potential

Pairs do **not** imply emotional, somatic, or symbolic response. They simply define the relationship architecture.

## Examples

| Pair Type              | Example                     | Notes                                      |
|------------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Relational             | Husband ↔ Wife             | Intimate / domestic relationship           |
| Professional           | Doctor ↔ Patient           | Clinical / caregiving relationship         |
| Power Dynamic          | Dominant ↔ Submissive     | Structured power exchange                  |
| Witness                | Observer ↔ Participant     | One-sided or mutual witnessing             |
| Human ↔ AI            | Human ↔ AI                | Human-AI relational context                |

## Related Layers

- **Mirrors**: Operate *across* the relationship defined by the Pair
- **Propagation**: Move resonance that originates across a Pair
- **Anatomical Sites**: Can participate in Pairs (e.g. body-to-body, body-to-object)

## Notes / Constraints

- Pairs create the *pathway*.
- Mirrors create the *resonance* across that pathway.
- Always define the Pair first before mapping Mirrors or Propagation.

## Example Usage

- Medical examination scenes (Doctor ↔ Patient)
- Caregiver / dependent dynamics
- Structured power exchange relationships
- Witness / participant configurations
